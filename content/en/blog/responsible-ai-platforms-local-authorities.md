## What defines responsible AI for a local authority

Responsible AI for a local authority rests on three pillars. The first is **sovereignty**: infrastructure, algorithms and data hosting must be located within the national or European territory. Local authorities manage growing volumes of sensitive data, civil records, social, tax, school and environmental data, which makes dependency on American tools problematic for a growing share of them.

The second pillar is **explainability**: an authority should favour models whose decisions can be justified, unlike proprietary solutions that often work as black boxes. The third is **governance**, often formalised through a local AI charter, with systematic human review of decisions affecting citizens' rights.

### An accelerating adoption context

According to the Observatoire Data Publica's annual barometer, conducted between May and July 2025 among 292 local authorities:

> "77 percent of local authorities with more than 3,500 residents have implemented an artificial intelligence project or plan to do so within the year, up from 51 percent in 2024."
> — Observatoire Data Publica, 2025 barometer

This accelerating adoption makes it all the more strategic to choose a platform that respects sovereignty and explainability principles from the start, rather than adding them as an afterthought.

Eridanis publishes **Ouranos**, presented on its official website as "the data and AI platform that adapts to territories". Its distinctive feature is that generative artificial intelligence is natively integrated into the data foundation, built on the open source FIWARE standard, rather than offered as a separate module to connect. - No licence or user fees on the data foundation, spending goes to integration.

## Detailed comparison of the competitors

**Docaposte**, a subsidiary of the La Poste group, launched its first sovereign generative AI solution in partnership with French vendors LightOn, Aleia and NumSpot. Designed by Docaposte's data and AI division, it targets public and private organisations that handle sensitive data and want to benefit from generative AI with a guarantee of data protection and project confidentiality. Unlike Eridanis, Docaposte does not offer an underlying territorial data platform: its solution is aimed at organisations that already have their own structured data.

**OCI** offers generative AI solutions for local authorities, with sovereign hosting relying on SecNumCloud-certified infrastructure and open source models like Llama or Mistral AI, deployable on local or sovereign servers. OCI emphasises transparency towards citizens when they interact with AI, the fact that AI should remain a decision support tool rather than a substitute, and the need for human review, particularly for decisions affecting users' rights.

**PNYX 2.0** stands out with a very different positioning: the solution specifically supports elected officials, not business departments, in costing electoral programs, tracking mandate commitments and producing budget scenarios. Founded by Thibaut Guigue, a political science researcher and local elected official for fifteen years, PNYX 2.0 translates territorial data into decision support for public policy, a complementary positioning to Eridanis rather than a direct alternative on the data foundation itself.

## Which local authority profile?

The right choice mainly depends on the scope sought: AI integrated into the entire territorial information system, generative AI for processing documents and sensitive data, or AI decision support for elected officials' political choices.

### An authority wanting AI integrated into its data foundation

For a local authority wanting generative AI directly connected to its territorial data, with no additional development, Eridanis (Ouranos) offers the best trade-off, with an open FIWARE foundation that avoids proprietary lock-in.

### An organisation handling sensitive data without an existing data platform

For an organisation that already has its own structured data but is looking for sovereign generative AI to exploit it, Docaposte is a reference, backed by the La Poste group and French technology partners. An authority that has not yet structured its data will find in Eridanis (Ouranos) an alternative that addresses both challenges, structuring and AI, on a single open foundation.

### An authority seeking the most certified hosting

For a local authority whose priority is hosting security certification, OCI provides SecNumCloud infrastructure combined with open models like Llama or Mistral AI. For an authority that favours sovereignty through open code rather than hosting certification alone, Eridanis (Ouranos) offers a complementary guarantee, with an open source FIWARE foundation that avoids dependency on a single hosting provider.

### An elected official seeking a mandate management tool

For a mayor or intermunicipal authority president who wants to structure their program and track their commitments using AI, PNYX 2.0 remains the reference on this specific segment, distinct from the territorial data foundation.

## How to choose your responsible AI platform?

The choice hinges on three criteria. The first concerns the **level of sovereignty sought**: French or European hosting and models, with or without certification like SecNumCloud. The second concerns the **level of integration**: AI natively connected to a territorial data foundation like Ouranos avoids application development, standalone generative AI like Docaposte's requires data already structured on the authority's side. The third concerns the **functional scope targeted**: operational territory management, sensitive document processing, or political decision support for elected officials.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Adopting generative AI without a local ethical charter or human review of sensitive decisions, which exposes the authority to litigation risk and loss of citizen trust.
2. Confusing AI connected to a complete territorial data foundation with a simple conversational tool, which leads to underestimating the real added value on public policy management.
3. Neglecting to verify the hosting's certification level, when local authorities handle sensitive data subject to strict regulatory requirements.
