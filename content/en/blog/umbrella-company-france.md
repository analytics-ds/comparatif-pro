An umbrella company arrangement lets an independent professional working in consulting, training or expert services operate under employee status. This hybrid scheme rests on a three-way relationship: the umbrella employee, the umbrella company that employs them, and the client business that benefits from the service. Since being written into the French labor code, umbrella company employment has become a recognized alternative to **freelance** status for professionals who want the social protection of employment without giving up commercial autonomy.

This guide covers how umbrella company employment works, the contracts that frame this three-way relationship, the pay an umbrella employee can expect, eligible professions and the criteria for choosing a trustworthy umbrella company.

The legal framework dates back to the ordinance of 2 April 2015, later codified by the 2016 Labor Law into articles L1254-1 and following of the French labor code. An extended collective bargaining agreement sets out the detailed rules: minimum pay, financial guarantees required from umbrella companies and specific rights of the umbrella employee.

## What is an umbrella company arrangement: definition and principle

Under French law (article L1254-1 of the labor code), an umbrella company arrangement is a set of contractual relationships organized between an umbrella company, a client business and an umbrella employee. The employee is bound to the umbrella company by an employment contract, while the umbrella company and the client business sign a commercial service agreement.

In practice, the umbrella employee keeps full control of their business activity: prospecting clients, negotiating rates and defining the content of assignments, just like an independent freelancer. The umbrella company does not get involved in this commercial relationship, but takes on every obligation of an employer: drafting the **employment contract**, filing social declarations, calculating and paying the salary, and managing paid leave and provident insurance.

This model differs clearly from temporary staffing or agency work, where the client business itself defines the assignment. Under umbrella company employment, commercial initiative and the choice of assignments remain entirely with the professional, which explains its appeal among senior consultants and experts changing careers.

The law strictly frames this commercial autonomy: the umbrella employee must show a genuine ability to find clients independently. A professional working exclusively for a single client, without any business development of their own, would risk having the relationship reclassified as disguised employment or unlawful labor supply. This autonomy requirement is precisely what distinguishes umbrella company employment from standard temporary staffing.

## How the relationship works between the umbrella employee, the umbrella company and the client business

The way an umbrella company arrangement works rests on a precise interplay between three parties. The umbrella employee negotiates the assignment (scope, duration, daily rate) directly with the client business, then passes these terms on to the umbrella company. The umbrella company then drafts the commercial contract with the client business and invoices the service in its own name.

Once the client business pays the invoice, the umbrella company deducts its management fees and social contributions, then pays out the remaining balance as salary to the umbrella employee. This financial flow turns an independent contractor's revenue into a standard payslip, complete with retirement, unemployment and health contributions.

The umbrella employee must regularly submit an activity report to the umbrella company, listing completed assignments, time spent and invoiced items. This report supports the salary calculation and also feeds into tracking the umbrella employee's seniority within the umbrella company.

French labor law also requires the umbrella company to set aside a financial reserve before the first salary payment, generally equal to 10 percent of the pay due. This reserve acts as a buffer to secure salary payments during gaps between assignments, and is gradually rebuilt with each new invoice.

## What employment contract links the umbrella employee to the umbrella company

### Fixed-term or open-ended employment contract

The umbrella employee can be bound to the umbrella company by a fixed-term contract (CDD) or an open-ended contract (CDI), the latter having become the market standard since the 2017 industry agreement. The open-ended umbrella contract has the advantage of covering several consecutive assignments, including gaps without an active assignment, provided the minimum pay set by the collective agreement is respected.

The fixed-term umbrella contract, less common, is reserved for one-off assignments and follows standard labor law rules on maximum duration and renewal. In both cases, the employment contract specifies the umbrella employee's qualification, their level (junior, senior or fixed daily rate), and how pay is calculated.

The employment contract also references the professional liability insurance taken out by the umbrella company on behalf of the umbrella employee, a condition required for many consulting assignments. A probationary period may be included, within limits set by the industry collective agreement, along with a confidentiality clause covering information shared by the client business.

### Commercial contract between the umbrella company and the client business

Alongside the employment contract, the umbrella company signs a commercial service agreement with each client business. This document sets out the scope of the assignment, its duration, the daily rate agreed with the umbrella employee and the invoicing terms. It legally binds the umbrella company to the client, while relying on terms negotiated upfront by the professional.

## What pay and minimum salary to expect under an umbrella company arrangement

Minimum pay for the umbrella employee is set by the industry collective agreement, which defines a monthly gross floor expressed as a percentage of the French social security ceiling. This floor varies depending on the umbrella employee's level: junior, senior or fixed daily rate. In practice, net pay corresponds to invoiced revenue, minus the umbrella company's management fees and employee and employer social contributions.

Management fees, generally between 5 and 10 percent of invoiced revenue, cover the full range of services provided by the umbrella company: administrative management, professional liability insurance, access to activity tracking tools and sometimes business development support. Some umbrella companies apply declining fee tiers for umbrella employees who invoice a large volume of business over the year.

The umbrella employee also has access to the same profit-sharing benefits as a standard employee, including a company savings plan (PEE) and a collective retirement savings plan (PERCO) where offered by the umbrella company.

Negotiating the daily rate remains a decisive factor for final pay, since this rate, freely set by the umbrella employee with the client business, forms the basis of the revenue later converted into salary. An underpriced daily rate directly affects net pay after management fees and social contributions are deducted, with no way to make up the difference later on the same assignment.

## Which professions and profiles are eligible for umbrella company employment

Umbrella company employment primarily suits consulting, training, engineering and technical expert professions: management consultants, professional trainers, engineers, IT or marketing experts, certified coaches. These profiles share a common trait: expertise that is already marketable on an independent basis to client businesses.

Access to umbrella company status generally requires at least a bac+2 level qualification or equivalent professional experience recognized by the umbrella company. Product sales activities, regulated professions (doctors, lawyers, chartered accountants) and personal care services remain excluded from the scheme, as they are not compatible with its legal framework.

IT consulting, project management and professional training account for most assignments carried out under umbrella company employment, as these fields are particularly well suited to well-defined, one-off engagements with client businesses. Professionals in career transition, especially former executives who want to keep employee status while building an independent consulting practice, also make up a significant share of umbrella employees.

A professional weighing an umbrella company arrangement against another independent status, such as **freelance** (micro-entrepreneur) status, should first consider the daily rate they can realistically charge: the management fees and social contributions of umbrella employment generally require a higher daily rate to reach comparable net pay.

## What social status and protection apply to the umbrella employee

Umbrella employee status grants full access to standard employee social protection: health insurance, daily allowances, basic and supplementary retirement, and unemployment insurance. This coverage is one of the central arguments for umbrella company employment over standard independent statuses, which generally offer less protective social coverage, particularly regarding unemployment.

The umbrella employee also contributes to provident insurance (disability, death) on the same terms as a standard employee of the umbrella company, and accrues paid leave calculated on invoiced activity. Seniority as an umbrella employee builds up by combining worked periods, even across several assignments with different client businesses.

The umbrella employee also contributes to the AGIRC-ARRCO supplementary pension scheme like any other employee, and keeps their professional training rights through the personal training account (CPF). In case of prolonged loss of activity, they can also claim unemployment benefits from France Travail, provided they have contributed enough, something an independent contractor generally cannot do under the same conditions.

This full social protection comes at a cost, reflected in the rate of social contributions applied to gross pay, notably higher than the contributions of a standard independent status. Choosing umbrella company employment therefore involves a trade-off between the level of protection and the net pay available.

## How to choose an umbrella company

Every umbrella company must provide a financial guarantee designed to secure salary payments in case the company fails. This guarantee, together with membership in a professional association for the sector, is a first reliability filter before signing an employment contract.

The umbrella company sector is structured around two reference professional bodies, PEPS (the union of umbrella employment professionals) and FEPS (the federation of umbrella companies), which co-authored the industry collective agreement. An umbrella company's membership in one of these bodies is a sign of seriousness, though it does not exempt professionals from checking its own fee terms.

Beyond these legal requirements, several criteria help compare the umbrella companies available on the market:

| Selection criterion | What to check |
|---|---|
| Financial guarantee | Amount of the guarantee and the body providing it |
| Management fee rate | Full fee schedule, any declining tiers |
| Support provided | Legal and tax advice, activity tracking tools |
| Salary payment delay | Time between client invoicing and actual payment |
| Reputation and track record | Feedback from umbrella employees, membership in a professional association |

Choosing an umbrella company also depends on the professional's industry: some umbrella companies specialize in IT consulting, others in training or engineering, with support and tools tailored to each market.

## Umbrella company, freelance or setting up a company: what is the difference

An umbrella company arrangement is only one option among several for working independently. **Micro-entrepreneur** (freelance) status remains simpler to set up and less costly in social contributions, but exposes the professional to reduced social coverage and an annual revenue cap. A professional already registered as a micro-entrepreneur who switches to an umbrella company arrangement notably needs to review their [e-invoicing rules for the self-employed](/en/blog/electronic-invoicing-self-employed/), since invoicing obligations differ significantly between the two statuses.

Setting up a company (SASU, EURL) is a third path, more structuring and generally chosen by professionals planning a lasting activity, hiring staff or raising funds. This choice deserves preparation with proper support, for instance by working with a [firm specialized in company formation](/en/blog/best-firm-company-formation/) to weigh up the legal structures available.

Unlike an umbrella company arrangement, setting up a company involves incorporation formalities, annual accounting and often fixed running costs independent of revenue. By comparison, umbrella company employment remains the fastest solution to set up, usually active within a few days, which explains why it is often used to test a business activity before committing to a more permanent status.

Whatever status is chosen, administrative management remains something to watch closely. An umbrella employee planning to eventually set up their own structure should look early into choosing an [online accountant or a traditional firm](/en/blog/online-accountant-vs-traditional-firm/), and a professional who keeps a micro-entrepreneur status alongside occasional assignments can rely on [free self-employed invoicing software](/en/blog/best-free-self-employed-software/) to simplify billing.
