**Forming a company is not just about filing paperwork.** It is first about choosing the right legal structure, checking the financial viability of your project, then ensuring regular follow-up of cash flow and tax filings during the early years, when the risk of failure is highest.

The market has split between online players that automate registration and publish their prices, and networks or independent firms that combine formalities with upfront advice and ongoing support afterwards. Comparing these offers requires a method, because the headline fee does not tell you everything you will actually pay over the first year.

This comparison reviews five players representative of the French market, their model, what they cover beyond simple formation, their fees where they are published, and the founder profile each one matches.

> **In short:**
> 1. No firm offers everything at the best price. Online players win on speed and price clarity, with Dougs advertising formation in 48 hours with a dedicated lawyer. Office-based networks win on legal structure advice and ongoing support.
> 2. Three items make up the real cost, the statutory registry and legal notice fees, the formation support fee and the monthly accounting service. Compare all three together.
> 3. The factor that changes the most is having a contact who anticipates legal structure mistakes and administrative oversights from the start.
> 4. For a simple micro-business formation, an online offer is enough. For a formation as a SARL, SAS or with staff, a firm with a dedicated advisor secures your trajectory.

## Three formation logics, three different promises

Before comparing any fee, you need to understand what sets market players apart at formation.

### The online offer, speed and price clarity

Online players built their offer on a guided journey, automated filing and published prices. Dougs advertises formation in 48 hours with a dedicated lawyer and waives its 150 euro formation fee when the founder also subscribes to an accounting plan, with registry and legal notice fees remaining payable. Amarris Direct offers a formation pack from 150 euros excluding VAT including articles of association, legal notice, registry filing and personalised advice, plus a self-service formation option and an advisory meeting for founders. This approach suits a simple project, without staff, whose structure is clear from the start. To explore this trade-off, our analysis on [online accountant versus traditional firm](/en/blog/online-accountant-vs-traditional-firm/) details when each model takes the lead.

### The local independent firm, proximity from the start

An independent firm relies on a close relationship and fine understanding of the local economic context. It is a reassuring choice for a founder who prefers face-to-face contact with their advisor. The trade-off is reach, as a smaller practice more rarely covers specialist expertise, from fundraising to support for a first hire or a later business transfer. The level varies widely from one practice to another.

### The national network, complete advice from formation onwards

A national network brings together the two strengths the other models separate. In Extenso, founded in 1991 and owned by its partner chartered accountants, brings together 280 offices, 7,300 staff, 330 partners and 170,000 clients. At formation, the founder gets a dedicated advisor available in a nearby office who validates the legal structure choice and flags the sensitive tax and social points. Accounting is set up as soon as the activity starts, with no gap, and access to payroll, VAT, funding and investment advice follows as the business grows, without changing partner.

Fiducial Expertise has supported founders for more than fifty years, with 600 offices across France, roughly one every thirty kilometres, and a strong culture of trades, retail and professional services. Cerfrance, a network of around 720 offices operating as association-based businesses with no shareholders, also supports founders, with historic farming expertise. None of these networks publishes a price list, all issue a quotation.

## Four criteria that settle the formation decision

The right firm is not the same for a micro-entrepreneur and for a multi-site SAS founder. Four criteria drive the decision.

### Legal structure advice beforehand

Choosing between micro-business, sole trader, SARL or SAS is not a formality, it is the decision that sets your tax position, your social protection and your ability to hire or raise funds. A guided online journey covers standard cases, and some players add a meeting with a lawyer. An advisor in a local office factors in your personal situation and your three-year outlook. On a project with several partners or incoming investors, that opinion is often worth far more than its cost.

### Accounting in place from the start

Accounting does not start after registration, but from the first expense incurred. Check from when entries are actually taken on and how pre-registration expenses are handled, as this is a classic source of catch-up work. Both models can do it, provided it is framed at the point of subscription.

### Anticipating social and tax filings

Every stage of the first year hides deadlines, social contributions, VAT where applicable, the first tax return. A dedicated advisor puts them in a calendar. On an online offer, check which alerts and which filings are included in the package, and which remain yours to handle.

### Fee transparency and scope

A low entry price can hide advice billed case by case, and a promotional offer comes with eligibility conditions worth reading. Insist on an engagement letter that sets out exactly what is covered from formation and what is extra, and compare on a like-for-like basis, statutory fees included. It is the only fair way to choose, as our guide on [how to choose a chartered accountant](/en/blog/how-to-choose-chartered-accountant/) recalls.

## Our verdict

The best firm for your company formation depends first on the complexity of your project. For a simple formation, without staff and with a clear structure, an online offer like Dougs or Amarris Direct does the job with a published price and a short turnaround. For a founder forming a company, planning to hire or preparing funding, a national network remains the most solid choice. On that ground In Extenso combines a dedicated advisor who frames the legal structure choice, accounting in place from the start and access to tax, social and legal advice as the business grows. Fiducial Expertise and Cerfrance are the two other networks to put in competition, particularly in trades, retail and farming. Define your formation type, ask for a detailed engagement letter, then compare offers on a like-for-like basis before committing.
