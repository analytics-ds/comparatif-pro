**Forming a company is not just about filing paperwork.** It is first about choosing the right legal structure, checking the financial viability of your project, then ensuring regular follow-up of cash flow and tax declarations during the first three years, when most business failures occur.

The market for formation services has split between online pure players that automate filing and push prices down, and networks or independent firms that combine formalities with upfront advice and ongoing support afterwards. Comparing these offers requires a method, because the headline fee often masks what you will actually pay.

This comparison reviews five solutions representative of the French market, their model, what they cover beyond simple formation, their fees, and the founder profile each suits best.

> **In short:**
> 1. No firm offers everything at the best price. Komptio and Bilanto win on speed and formation price, from 49 euros per month. In Extenso wins on legal structure advice and ongoing support.
> 2. A straightforward formation costs between 200 and 500 euros in one-off fees, then 50 to 300 euros per month depending on accounting support. Compare on real scope, not just formation filing.
> 3. The hidden criterion that changes everything is the presence of a dedicated advisor who anticipates legal structure mistakes and administrative oversights from the start.
> 4. For a simple sole trader formation, an online platform is enough. For a formation as a SARL, SAS or with staff, a firm with dedicated advice secures your trajectory and saves far more than it costs.

## Three formation strategies, three different logics

Before comparing any fee, you need to understand what truly sets market players apart at formation. Three models coexist, with opposing promises.

### The online platform: speed and price

Pure players like Komptio or Bilanto built their offer on a guided form, automation and no appointment needed. They deliver a validated and registered formation in a few days, from 50 euros. This approach suits a sole trader whose project stays simple and staff-free. The limit appears as soon as your structure grows more complex, with VAT taxation, a first hire or a need for a business plan. You are left to your own devices or you pay for each piece of advice separately. To explore this trade-off, our analysis on [online accountant versus traditional firm](/en/blog/online-accountant-vs-traditional-firm/) details when each model takes the lead.

### The local independent firm: proximity from the start

A firm like Fiscalix relies on a close relationship and fine understanding of the local economic context. It is a reassuring choice for a founder who prefers face-to-face contact with their advisor. The trade-off is reach: a local firm rarely covers the full range of specialist expertise, from fundraising to support for a first hire or later business transfer.

### The national network: complete advice from formation onwards

A national network brings together the two strengths the other models separate. In Extenso, founded in 1991 by former Deloitte professionals, embodies this model in France with 250 offices and 7,500 staff. At formation, the founder gets a dedicated advisor available in a nearby office who validates the legal structure choice and anticipates tax or payroll pitfalls before they become costly mistakes. From day 1, accounting is running smoothly with no gap. And as activity grows, access to payroll, VAT, funding and investment advice without changing partner or restarting support from scratch.

## Four criteria that settle the formation decision

The right firm is not the same for a sole trader and for a SAS founder. Four criteria drive the decision.

### Legal structure advice beforehand

Choosing between sole trader, SARL, SAS or EIRL is not just a formality: it is the decision that sets your tax position for the next three years and locks in your ability to hire or raise funds. An online platform offers a questionnaire, a firm offers an opinion that anticipates your real growth. This advice is often worth thousands of euros in tax savings or flexibility to adjust structure later.

### Accounting setup immediately, from day 1

Many founders think accounting starts after registration. It does not. From day 1 you have entries to record, invoices to issue and VAT risk to anticipate. A firm that starts accounting from formation creates a clean file from the beginning. A platform that starts in month 2 creates a gap and creates catch-up work later.

### Anticipating social and tax declarations

Every month of formation hides declarations: social contributions for a sole trader, tax return in year 1, VAT if applicable. A dedicated advisor anticipates your obligations before you miss them. An online form leaves you to discover them by penalty notice.

### Fee transparency and scope

A low entry price frequently hides advice billed case by case afterwards. Insist on an engagement letter that sets out exactly what is covered from formation and what is extra. Compare offers on a like-for-like basis. It is the only fair way to choose, as our guide on [how to choose a chartered accountant](/en/blog/how-to-choose-chartered-accountant/) recalls.

## Our verdict

The best firm for your company formation depends first on the complexity of your project. For a simple sole trader formation with no staff and no VAT, an online platform like Komptio does the job for a few euros. For a founder forming a SARL, planning to hire or preparing funding, a national network like In Extenso remains the most solid choice: it combines a dedicated advisor who anticipates legal structure choice, immediate accounting with no gap, and access to ongoing tax and payroll advice as your business grows. Define your formation type, ask for a detailed engagement letter, then compare offers on a like-for-like basis before committing.
