**ISO 9001 certification** confirms that an organization has a quality management system that meets the requirements of an internationally recognized standard. Backed by the International Organization for Standardization, it applies to large industrial groups as much as to service-sector SMEs looking to formalize their processes and reassure their clients or contracting parties. The process involves internal preparation, the choice of an accredited body, and an audit that determines whether the certificate is issued.

This guide covers the steps of certification, its actual cost, the changes introduced by the 2026 revision of the standard, and the criteria for choosing a certification body.

## What is ISO 9001 certification?

The ISO 9001 standard sets out the requirements for a **quality management system** (QMS). It does not focus on a specific product or service, but on how an organization manages its processes to consistently satisfy customers and meet applicable regulatory requirements.

Certification is based on the process approach: every activity within the company is described, its interactions with other processes are identified, and indicators are used to measure its performance. This approach replaced the older, more rigid procedural approach of the first versions of the standard published in the 1990s.

Seven quality management principles structure the standard: customer focus, leadership, engagement of people, process approach, continual improvement, evidence-based decision making, and relationship management with interested parties. A certified company must demonstrate, at each audit, that these principles translate into concrete practice within its organization.

The standard also relies on the **PDCA** logic (Plan, Do, Check, Act), which structures continual improvement in four stages: planning objectives and resources, carrying out the planned actions, checking the results against the objectives, then adjusting the system accordingly. This loop repeats at every certification cycle, which is what distinguishes a living quality approach from a one-off compliance exercise.

ISO 9001 can be combined with other management standards, notably ISO 14001 for the environment and ISO 45001 for occupational health and safety. Companies pursuing a comprehensive **QSE** (Quality, Safety, Environment) certification then share much of their documentation and audits, which reduces the marginal cost of each additional standard once the first system is in place.

## The steps of ISO 9001 certification

The certification process generally follows six steps, spread over six to eighteen months depending on the organization's maturity:

1. **Initial gap assessment**: evaluating the gaps between existing practices and the requirements of the standard.
2. **Designing the quality management system**: drafting processes, mandatory procedures, and the quality policy.
3. **Rollout and training**: getting teams to adopt the system, setting up monitoring indicators.
4. **Mock audit**: an optional but recommended internal check before the official audit.
5. **Two-stage certification audit**: a documentary audit checks the system's conformity on paper, then an on-site audit checks its actual application.
6. **Certification decision and follow-up**: the certificate is issued, valid for three years, with an annual surveillance audit and a full renewal audit at expiry.

A minor nonconformity noted during the audit generally does not prevent certification, provided a corrective action plan is submitted within the deadline set by the auditor. A major nonconformity, on the other hand, suspends the decision until it is shown to have been resolved.

## ISO 9001 version 2026: what changes

The transition to the revised version of the standard, under way since 2026, strengthens several requirements already present in the High Level Structure (HLS) shared by all management system standards. Organizations already certified have a transition period to adapt their system before their surveillance or renewal audits incorporate the new requirements.

The changes mainly concern stronger formalization of risk and opportunity management, increased requirements for digital traceability of compliance evidence, and clarified expectations regarding the organization's external context, including climate and regulatory issues.

Companies already certified do not need to restart their entire process: a transition audit, usually combined with the annual surveillance audit, is enough to check compliance with the new requirements. Certification bodies communicate a precise schedule in advance so this transition can be organized without any interruption to the certificate's validity.

## How much does ISO 9001 certification cost?

The cost of ISO 9001 certification depends on three main factors: the company's headcount, the number of sites to be audited, and the complexity of its processes. For an SME with around thirty employees on a single site, the budget usually falls between 3,000 and 8,000 euros for the initial audit, plus lower-cost annual surveillance audits.

This amount covers only the certification body's service. It does not include the **internal time** spent preparing the quality management system, nor the potential cost of an external consultant, which can represent an equivalent or higher budget depending on the organization's initial level of maturity.

Several funding schemes can ease this burden, notably through regional chambers of commerce and industry or sector-specific calls for projects. Organizations whose quality teams take a [certified management training](/en/blog/certified-management-training/) program alongside the certification process generally see faster adoption of the system, which limits the need for prolonged external support.

## Which certification body should you choose?

The choice of certification body should primarily favor an organization accredited by **Cofrac** (the French accreditation committee) or an equivalent, internationally recognized accreditation body. This accreditation guarantees that the certificate issued will be recognized by clients, contracting parties and public authorities.

| Body | Accreditation | Average lead time | Indicative price (SME) | Specificity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AFNOR Certification | Cofrac | 6 to 9 months | 4,000-8,000 € | Historic reference, strong reputation in France |
| Bureau Veritas | Cofrac | 6 to 9 months | 4,500-9,000 € | International presence, multi-standard |
| SGS | Cofrac | 6 to 9 months | 4,000-8,500 € | Strong industrial and export footprint |
| DEKRA | Cofrac | 5 to 8 months | 3,500-7,500 € | Competitive pricing for SMEs |
| LRQA | Cofrac | 6 to 10 months | 4,500-9,500 € | Sector expertise in energy and environment |

The prices shown remain indicative ranges: each body issues a quote after a preliminary questionnaire covering headcount, number of sites and the intended certification scope. Beyond price, auditor availability, their familiarity with the industry, and the quality of guidance provided during the audit are equally decisive selection criteria.

## Documentation and requirements of the quality management system

The ISO 9001 standard requires a minimum set of documentation, even though it leaves considerable freedom regarding its form. The documents expected during an audit include the quality policy, the scope of the management system, the process map, evidence of management review, and records related to nonconformities and corrective actions.

Drafting this **technical documentation** clearly and usably largely determines how smoothly the audit runs. A well-structured [technical documentation guide](/en/blog/how-to-create-technical-documentation/) helps avoid redundancy, makes it easier to update procedures, and saves valuable time during annual surveillance audits.

The team responsible for running the quality management system also benefits from project management skills to coordinate audit preparation across several departments. [Recognized project management certifications](/en/blog/project-management-training-certifications/) provide a useful methodological framework for structuring this kind of cross-functional effort, particularly in mid-sized organizations.

## The benefits of ISO 9001 certification for a company

ISO 9001 certification produces measurable effects that go beyond formal compliance. It durably structures the organization and serves as a commercial argument in many public and private tenders, where it sometimes acts as an eliminatory criterion.

- **Stronger customer satisfaction**: indicator-based monitoring makes it possible to identify issues earlier and respond to them in a structured way.
- **Lower cost of non-quality**: formalizing processes reduces recurring errors and rework.
- **Easier access to public tenders and major accounts**: certification is frequently required or valued in supplier selection criteria.
- **Internal cohesion**: clarifying roles and processes reduces areas of ambiguity between departments.
- **Credibility abroad**: the international recognition of the standard eases relationships with foreign clients or partners.

These benefits only materialize, however, if the quality management system stays active after the certificate is obtained, with regular management reviews and genuine ownership by teams, beyond simply preparing for the audit.
