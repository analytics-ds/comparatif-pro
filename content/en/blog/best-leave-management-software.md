## What is leave management software and why equip your business with it

**Leave management software** centralises absence requests, automatically calculates balances based on the applicable collective agreement, and organises the approval workflow between the employee and their manager. It replaces spreadsheet tracking, often a source of calculation errors and poorly managed processing delays.

For a small business or an SME, leave management touches payroll, labour law and the day-to-day organisation of teams. A balance calculation error or a missed approval can lead to a dispute with an employee or an anomaly on a pay slip.

### The threshold beyond which a spreadsheet is no longer enough

The tipping point is generally around 15 employees, the threshold where the complexity of manual tracking (multiple leave types, seniority, splitting, time savings accounts) becomes hard to maintain without errors. A payroll administrator spends on average between 5 and 10 hours a month re-entering into the payroll software information already captured elsewhere, with an anomaly rate of 15 to 20 percent on the pay slips concerned.

## The criteria for choosing the right solution

The choice of this tool depends on the company's size, the complexity of the applicable agreement-based rules, and the level of integration wanted with payroll. A 20-employee company with a single collective agreement does not have the same requirements as an industrial group managing several sites, variable schedules and distinct company agreements per site.

### The features that make the difference

- **Online requests and a mobile app**: the employee submits their absence request directly from their smartphone, without a paper form.
- **Multi-level approval workflow**: the request follows an approval path matching the company's actual reporting structure.
- **Automatic balance calculation**: the software applies the collective agreement, seniority and splitting rules without manual intervention.
- **Payroll integration**: approved absences flow directly into the payroll software, without re-entry.
- **Absenteeism dashboard**: tracking trends by team to anticipate understaffing periods.

## Why automating leave management changes daily HR work

An HR team managing leave on a spreadsheet spends a disproportionate amount of time on repetitive tasks: copying requests, checking balances, chasing managers for approval. A study conducted by Éditions Tissot and PayFit in March 2022 measured the scale of this administrative burden:

> "72 percent of HR professionals spend between 3 and 6 hours of their day on administrative tasks."
> — Éditions Tissot and PayFit, March 2022

Leave management software does not eliminate this burden entirely, but it automates a significant share of it: balance calculation, approval workflow, payroll integration. The HR time freed up can then be redirected towards higher-value tasks, such as absenteeism tracking or team support.

## How to choose and roll out the right solution

1. **Map the applicable agreement-based rules**: collective agreement, company agreements, seniority and splitting rules the software needs to enforce.
2. **Check compatibility with the existing payroll software**, to avoid manually re-entering approved absences, a point to cross-reference with our [payroll software comparison](/en/blog/best-payroll-software/).
3. **Test the employee request journey**, on both mobile and desktop, before rolling the tool out company-wide.
4. **Configure approval workflows** based on the actual reporting structure, not a simplified theoretical chart.
5. **Monitor the first months of use** to correct gaps between the configured rules and the edge cases encountered in practice.
6. **Train managers on the approval workflow**, often the most frequent friction point during rollout, even ahead of technical issues on the employee side.

A gradual rollout, by team or by site, limits blockages and allows rules to be adjusted before a company-wide generalisation. This staged approach also reduces the support load on HR, who can handle questions from a small population before opening the tool to the entire workforce.
