## What is document management software and why equip your business with it

**Document management software** (DMS) centralises the capture, filing, search and electronic archiving of a company's documents: invoices, contracts, letters, internal notes, technical sheets. It replaces paper filing or unstructured shared folders, where a poorly named or poorly filed document becomes untraceable within a few weeks.

For a small business or an SME, document management touches both daily productivity and regulatory compliance. An invoice or contract that cannot be found during an audit can be costly, while a document search that takes several minutes each time adds up to significant lost time over a year.

### The difference between a shared folder and a real DMS

A standard shared folder relies on a file hierarchy and on each user's discipline to correctly name and file their documents. A DMS adds a layer of structure: optical character recognition (OCR) to make scanned documents searchable, metadata indexing, fine-grained access rights, and often legally-binding archiving for documents that must be kept with a legal guarantee.

## The criteria for choosing the right document management software

The choice depends on the volume of documents processed, the level of regulatory compliance sought and the internal technical resources available.

### The features that make the difference

- **Capture and OCR**: scanning and automatic text recognition to make scanned documents searchable in full text.
- **Automatic filing**: documents filed into virtual binders according to predefined rules, without systematic manual intervention.
- **Validation workflows**: configurable approval process for documents that require validation before final archiving.
- **Legally-binding archiving**: legal document retention according to standards like NF Z42-013, with timestamping and integrity guarantees.
- **Full-text search**: finding a document from its content, not just its file name.

## Why document structuring matters for regulatory compliance

Several French regulatory obligations rely on a company's ability to produce a precise document, at a precise date, with an integrity guarantee. This is notably the case for invoice archiving, whose legal retention period requires being able to retrieve a document several years after it was issued. A DMS with legally-binding archiving, certified to the NF Z42-013 standard, directly addresses this issue, unlike a simple shared folder that guarantees neither the integrity nor the traceability of documents over time.

This compliance requirement fits into a broader movement of digitising administrative exchanges, which our [e-invoicing guide](/en/blog/electronic-invoicing-guide/) details the obligations applicable to businesses. A company that structures its DMS before a new regulatory obligation takes effect often avoids a rushed compliance project, with the costs and error risks that involves.

## How to choose and roll out your DMS

1. **Map the types of documents to manage**: invoices, contracts, letters, internal notes, each with its own retention and search requirements.
2. **Check the level of legally-binding archiving needed**, particularly for documents subject to a legal retention period.
3. **Test OCR quality on the company's real documents**, before rolling the tool out company-wide, as text recognition varies depending on scan quality.
4. **Configure validation workflows** based on the company's actual organisation, not a simplified theoretical chart.
5. **Plan the migration of existing documents**, often the longest task of a DMS rollout, particularly when documents are scattered across several shared folders.
6. **Train teams on the new document workflow**, to avoid part of the documents continuing to bypass the tool, which would defeat the purpose of the DMS.

A gradual rollout, by document type or by department, limits blockages and allows filing rules to be adjusted before a company-wide generalisation.
