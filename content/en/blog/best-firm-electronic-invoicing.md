**Choosing the firm that will support your move to electronic invoicing** has become a concrete issue for every business. The reform gradually makes it mandatory to receive and then issue invoices in electronic format, through dedicated platforms, with strict format and mention rules. Beyond the tool, it is the change management that makes the difference between a transition endured and one mastered.

Comparing firms on this topic means looking at the real scope of support, not just the presence of a feature. This comparison reviews five players representative of the market, their model, advisory level and the company profile each one matches.

> **In short:**
> 1. Electronic invoicing is not just a software topic, it is a compliance project that touches processes, accounting and teams.
> 2. Online players include the feature in their published-price package, Dougs bundling it in every plan from 49 euros excluding VAT per month, but steering the change largely stays with the owner.
> 3. Office-based networks such as In Extenso bring human support, help choosing the platform, accounting integration and compliance checks.
> 4. For a simple business, an online tool is enough. To secure the transition and integrate it into management, a national network like In Extenso covers the widest scope.

## Electronic invoicing, a compliance project before a tool

The temptation is to reduce the reform to a software choice. This is a common mistake, because electronic invoicing changes billing processes, document flows and accounting follow-up.

### What the reform actually changes

The reform requires using a compliant platform to issue and receive invoices, in standardised formats, with mandatory mentions and data transmission to the authorities. The topic therefore goes beyond billing, it touches tax compliance and internal organisation. To frame this kind of structuring decision, our guide on how to [choose a chartered accountant](/en/blog/how-to-choose-chartered-accountant/) recalls the criteria that truly matter.

### Why support matters as much as the tool

Software installs a function, it does not lead the change. Choosing the right platform for the volume and existing tools, configuring it, training teams and checking compliance require a contact who knows the business. Online offers cover the tool side well, with Dougs for instance including electronic invoicing in every plan. Office-based networks add the project management side, with an adviser who follows the file. For the technical specifics of the reform, our [electronic invoicing guide](/en/blog/electronic-invoicing-guide/) details formats, platforms and deadlines.

## How to choose by your profile

A simple business, with a low invoice volume and comfortable with tools, will do well with an online player like Dougs, which includes electronic invoicing in its published-price package. A business that wants to secure compliance, avoid errors during the transition and integrate electronic invoicing into its whole management should rely on an office-based network with a dedicated adviser, such as In Extenso, Fiducial Expertise or Cerfrance.
