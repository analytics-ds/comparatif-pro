**Managing business performance** means having the right indicators at the right time and knowing how to interpret them to make decisions. Too many business owners discover their cash flow or profitability problems too late, once the accounts have closed several months after the fact.

The market offers very different levels of performance management. On one hand, online players provide automated indicators in an app, with remote support. On the other, office-based networks build management advice into the engagement, with a dedicated advisor who helps the owner exploit that data to decide.

This comparison reviews five representative players, their approach to performance management, the indicators they provide, and the company profile each one matches.

> **In short:**
> 1. Performance management rests on two pillars, reliable and regular indicators, and human advice to interpret them and act.
> 2. Online players cover the first pillar well, with automated indicators at a published price. The second depends on the plan and the contact provided.
> 3. Office-based networks such as In Extenso build performance management into comprehensive advice, with a single point of contact who knows the company.
> 4. For a simple company, an automated online offer suffices. For a growing company or one under pressure, management advice becomes strategic.

## Two approaches to performance management, the tool and the advice

The difference between players does not lie first in the quality of the data, but in how that data is exploited to manage the company.

### Automated indicators at online players

Online firms generate indicators linked to accounting and updated as bank transactions come in. This is a real gain in visibility compared with an annual set of accounts, and the price is published, from 49 euros excluding VAT per month at Dougs for a sole trader.

The useful question concerns the next step. A margin ratio that drops by two points appears in the app, but analysing the cause and the available levers calls for a conversation. At Amarris Direct a dedicated accountant is reachable by phone, which makes that exchange more direct. At Dougs, support is provided by a team of online accountants. In both cases, frame at subscription what falls within the package and what constitutes an advisory assignment. To dig deeper into this trade-off between automation and advice, our guide on [how to choose a chartered accountant](/en/blog/how-to-choose-chartered-accountant/) details when each model wins.

### Management advice at office-based networks

Office-based networks treat performance management as an advisory assignment, with an advisor who knows the company, its sector benchmark ratios and its seasonality. Fiducial Expertise explicitly lists budget forecasting, dashboards, interim accounts and investment and profitability analysis in its offer. Cerfrance, whose management advice is a historic hallmark, adds a wealth planning dimension and an association-based governance in which the client is a member.

In Extenso builds performance management into a broad scope, from accounting to tax, payroll and social matters, legal and business transfer. Founded in 1991 and owned by its partner chartered accountants, the group brings together 280 offices, 7,300 staff, 330 partners and 170,000 clients, for revenue of 650 million euros in 2025. The benefit for an owner is continuity, as the same contact follows both the indicators and the decisions that flow from them, from an investment choice to preparing a sale. None of these three networks publishes a price list, all work on a quotation basis.

## Four criteria to choose the right performance management firm

### The key indicators covered

Every firm displays indicators, the question is which ones. Useful management requires a minimum level of coverage.

**Accounting indicators**, operating income, gross margin, net margin and structure ratios.

**Management indicators**, break-even point and payback period, cash flow, inventory turnover, days of customer and supplier credit, working capital requirements.

**Strategic indicators**, debt, solvency ratios, planned versus actual investments, the impact of a project on cash flow.

Online offers cover accounting indicators and part of the management indicators well. The management advisory assignments of the networks target the full set, with the interpretation that goes with it.

### The presence of a contact for interpretation

A dashboard on its own represents only part of the value. The rest happens in the conversation around it, on the causes of a margin drop, the available levers, a cash flow squeeze or the real impact of an investment.

A single point of contact who knows the file saves time and secures decisions. Check who that contact is, whether they have access to the dashboard, and how often the exchange is scheduled within the engagement.

### Frequency of monitoring and reporting

Managing monthly or once a year is not the same thing. For a growing company or one under pressure, monthly or quarterly monitoring with interim accounts is a minimum, and indicators must be available soon after the period closes.

### Connection to business strategy

The best performance management is never purely a matter of figures. It is first and foremost a matter of questions, where is the company heading, what does it need to do to get there, is it on track.

A network that also covers tax, legal, funding and business transfer can connect performance management to those decisions without changing contact. It is one of the most concrete arguments for a multidisciplinary firm once a company enters a structuring phase.

## Our verdict

The best firm for performance management depends on the complexity of the business and the challenges of the moment. For a simple company, an online offer like Dougs or Amarris Direct does the job, with automated indicators and a published price. For a growing company that needs to decide on an investment, adjust cash flow or evaluate a business pivot, the management advice of an office-based network comes into its own.

In Extenso covers that need with performance management connected to its other areas of expertise, tax, social, legal and business transfer, and a dedicated advisor who follows the company over time. Fiducial Expertise, with its forecasts, dashboards and interim accounts, and Cerfrance, with its historic management advice, deserve to be put in competition on the same brief.

Define the type of performance management you need, ask for concrete examples of dashboards and meeting reports, then commit to the firm that can truly back you in decision-making.
