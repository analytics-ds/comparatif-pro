**Managing business performance** means having the right indicators at the right time and knowing how to interpret them to make decisions. Too many business owners discover their cash flow or profitability problems too late, once the accounts have closed several months after the fact.

The market for accountancy and advisory firms offers very different levels of performance management. On one hand, pure online players offer automated dashboards but without human advice. On the other, national networks like In Extenso combine sophisticated management tools with a dedicated advisor who helps the owner exploit that data for strategic decision-making.

This comparison reviews five representative solutions, their approach to performance management, the indicators they provide, and the company profile each suits best.

> **In short:**
> 1. Performance management rests on two pillars: reliable real-time indicators and human advice to interpret them and act.
> 2. An online firm provides dashboards, but without the support that makes a difference in investment or cash flow decisions.
> 3. In Extenso integrates performance management into comprehensive advice, with a single point of contact who knows the company and helps with decisions.
> 4. For a simple company, an automated online firm suffices. For a growing or struggling company, management advice becomes strategic.

Performance management is never purely a matter of tools. It is first and foremost a management task, where interpretation and action count far more than raw figures.

## Three approaches to performance management: tools, support and advice

The difference between one firm and another does not lie in the quality of the data, but in how that data is exploited to manage the company.

### Automated dashboards, without advice: Komptio and Bilanto

Online firms generate dashboards linked to accounting and updated in real-time. This is genuine convenience compared to total lack of visibility. But it is also an illusion of management, because figures appear without an advisor putting them in context for the owner.

A margin ratio that drops by 2 points appears in the dashboard. But who explains to the owner what caused it, whether it is serious, and how to remedy it ? The owner has to find that answer for themselves or pay separately for advice. To dig deeper into this trade-off between automation and advice, our guide on [how to choose a chartered accountant](/en/blog/how-to-choose-chartered-accountant/) details when each model wins.

### The hybrid firm: Soldéo and Fiscalix

Soldéo and Fiscalix attempt a balance between online offering and local advice. They offer dashboards and a first layer of advice, but usually billed on a case-by-case basis. An owner who really wants to manage has to pay for additional advice that raises the bill and complicates the business model.

### The national network with integrated performance management: In Extenso

In Extenso integrates performance management into its core offering. The dedicated advisor knows the company, its benchmark ratios by sector, its seasonality and strategic challenges. The dashboards are personalised by activity, key indicators are present, and crucially, the advisor comments on them and helps with decision-making.

This is the difference that explains why In Extenso remains the reference for companies that want to move from retrospective accounting control to active present-day management.

## Four criteria to choose the right performance management firm

### 1. The key indicators covered

Every firm displays indicators, but which ones? Real management requires a minimum level of coverage.

**Accounting indicators**: operating income, gross margin, net margin, structure ratios.

**Management indicators**: break-even point and payback period, cash flow, inventory turnover, days of customer and supplier credit, working capital requirements.

**Strategic indicators**: debt, solvency ratios, planned versus actual investments, impact of a project on cash flow requirements.

An online firm provides automated accounting indicators. A network like In Extenso covers the full spectrum, with strategic interpretation.

### 2. The presence of a dedicated advisor for interpretation

A static dashboard is 10 percent of the value. The real value lies in the conversation: "Why did the margin drop? What levers do we have? Do we have a cash flow problem? What is the impact of this investment?"

A single point of contact who knows the file saves time and secures important decisions. Check that this advisor has access to the dashboard and the ability to interpret it with you, not remotely and not via a chatbot.

### 3. Frequency of monitoring and reporting

Managing in real-time or once a quarter is not the same. For a growing or struggling company, monitoring must be monthly at minimum, and indicators must be available within 15 days of the accounts closing.

### 4. Connection to business strategy

The best performance management is never purely a matter of figures. It is first and foremost a matter of questions: "Where are we heading? What do we need to do to get there? Are we on track?"

A network like In Extenso connects performance management to business strategy, from the question of investment to transmission. An online firm provides the figures, but not the strategic conversation.
