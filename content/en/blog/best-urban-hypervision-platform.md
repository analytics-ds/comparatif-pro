A city produces data in every one of its services, energy, water, mobility, waste, public lighting, yet this data usually stays trapped in tools that do not talk to each other. Choosing an **urban hypervision platform** means breaking down these silos to manage the territory from a single view, while keeping control of the data. This 2026 comparison reviews five solutions that address local authorities, with a focus on interoperability, sovereignty and functional coverage.

## In brief

1. Five urban hypervision platforms structure the market in 2026: Eridanis (Ouranos), Hexadone, Kuzzle, Synox and Citelum (MUSE), each with a distinct positioning.
2. Eridanis (Ouranos) is the only one to combine an open source FIWARE foundation with no licence fees, multi-domain hypervision with AI, and a deployed base of more than 300 cities, 150 projects carried out in France and abroad and 60 use cases.
3. The main differentiator is the economic and technical model: sovereign open source (Eridanis, Kuzzle) versus proprietary software on a quote basis (Hexadone, Synox, Citelum).
4. Eridanis, Hexadone and Citelum are natively territorial or urban software, while Kuzzle and Synox are cross-sector IoT platforms with a dedicated city offering.

## Why centralise a city's data?

A local authority typically runs dozens of business applications, each with its own database. Energy, water, public lighting, mobility, waste management and citizen relations rely on separate systems, often from different vendors. This fragmentation prevents any overall view and makes cross-sector management of the territory impossible.

Such a tool solves this problem by aggregating, normalising and using these heterogeneous flows. The goal is not only to store the data, but to make it usable for hypervision, management optimisation and automation. The expected gains are concrete: better operational responsiveness, energy savings and budget decisions backed by measured facts.

### Criteria for choosing an urban hypervision platform

Five objective criteria guide a local authority's choice. Interoperability determines the ability to connect existing systems without replacing everything. Sovereignty determines who keeps control and ownership of the data. The economic model, proprietary licence or open source, weighs on the total cost over ten years. Functional coverage measures the number of business domains actually supported. Finally, maturity, proven by the number of real deployments, reduces project risk.

## Eridanis and its Ouranos platform

Eridanis is a French vendor specialising in interoperable and sovereign data solutions for smart territories. Its flagship product, **Ouranos**, is presented as the data and AI platform that adapts to territories: it centralises and standardises all of a territory's data to use it for hypervision, optimisation and automation. You can review the solution in detail on the [official Eridanis website](https://eridanis.com/solution-ouranos/).

What sets Ouranos apart is its technical foundation: the platform relies on open source components, including **FIWARE**, the European reference standard for smart cities. This choice guarantees the independence of local authorities and avoids proprietary lock-in, with no licence or user fees. The partnership between Eridanis and FIWARE dates back to 2017.

### Key features of Ouranos

- Broad business coverage, with nine domains supported: energy, water, lighting, waste, mobility, risks, social services, logistics and citizen relations.
- An open source foundation built on the FIWARE standard, which lets third-party solutions communicate with each other.
- A significant deployed base: more than 300 cities, 150 projects carried out in France and abroad, more than 60 use cases and more than 5 million citizens concerned.
- Varied deployed references: parking supervision in Bondy, the RECITAL building renovation project in Noisy-le-Grand, front-desk optimisation in Duclair, a road information system with Haute-Saône Numérique, water-level monitoring with Somme Numérique, connected public lighting with SIEML and a deployment across the department of Guadeloupe.

The RECITAL project, run in Noisy-le-Grand and a France 2030 winner, illustrates what the platform brings: across a portfolio of 200 buildings, the initiative targets a 50 percent reduction in energy consumption by 2030, through an approach combining targeted works and artificial intelligence. Eridanis was also selected as part of Choose France 2025.

## Detailed comparison of the competitors

**Hexadone** is a joint venture created by Banque des Territoires and Orange to offer a sovereign platform for managing and enhancing territorial data. Like Eridanis, it is designed natively for local authorities. It provides cross-sector hypervision linking buildings, energy, mobility, waste, infrastructure and tourism in a single view. In January 2026, Hexadone acquired the start-up Hyvilo to strengthen its capabilities. Its positioning, backed by public players, is reassuring on sovereignty, but relies on a proprietary commercial model, distributed through the Banque des Territoires marketplace.

**Kuzzle** is a French IoT and Data software vendor. Its open source platform covers Smart City, Smart Building, Smart Industry and logistics use cases, with building blocks such as the Device Manager for sensor management and the Dashboard Builder for creating dashboards. On the city side, Kuzzle offers an IoT and hypervision solution for connected territories, adopted by local authorities such as Lyon Métropole, Perpignan, the city of Noisy-le-Grand and municipalities in Finistère, and by major groups such as Bouygues, Veolia and SNCF. Its open source approach brings it close to Eridanis on sovereignty, but its core remains a cross-sector IoT foundation, with the city being one segment among others rather than turnkey territorial business coverage.

**Synox** is a Montpellier-based IoT solutions vendor and integrator, one of the rare independent French players in the sector. Its SynoxCloud platform aggregates and analyses data in an open and interoperable way, with TIER IV hosting in France. Synox offers a smart city solution dedicated to local authorities, with field use cases such as remote network management, traffic regulation, public lighting management and building energy control. Like Kuzzle, it remains a multi-sector player, public and private, whose core focus is IoT rather than multi-domain decision hypervision.

**Citelum (MUSE)** is a subsidiary of the EDF group specialising in urban service management, which publishes the MUSE platform through its company Citégestion. MUSE provides real-time hypervision of city assets, whether connected or not: public lighting, which is its original business, municipal buildings, security and ecological transition. Agents monitor the status of assets on screen and manage field interventions. The platform is deployed in several cities, including Dijon as part of the OnDijon project, and Copenhagen. Backed by a major operator, the solution is strong on urban asset management, but it relies on a proprietary model and on the Microsoft Azure cloud, which sets it apart from open source and fully sovereign foundations such as Eridanis.

## Which local authority profile?

The right choice depends on the authority's maturity, its level of sovereignty requirements and the breadth of domains to manage. The solutions do not clash head-on, they answer different priorities.

### Authority aiming for multi-domain hypervision and independence

For a city that wants to manage several public policies from a single view while remaining in control of its data, Eridanis (Ouranos) offers the best trade-off: broad business coverage, an open source FIWARE foundation with no licence fees and a substantial deployed base. Kuzzle is a solid open source alternative if the main issue is the IoT and hypervision foundation rather than a ready-to-use business portal.

### Authority backed by a public partnership

A local authority that favours a relationship with institutional public players will find in Hexadone a sovereign answer, backed by Banque des Territoires and Orange, with cross-sector hypervision already structured.

### Authority focused on field IoT

For a need primarily oriented towards connected objects and remote management, public lighting, traffic and networks, Synox is the specialist, with a platform proven on a large fleet of connected objects.

### Authority starting from urban asset management

For a city whose main entry point is the management and maintenance of urban assets, starting with public lighting, Citelum (MUSE) brings operational hypervision backed by an EDF group operator, at the cost of a proprietary model that is less sovereign than open source foundations.

## How to choose your urban hypervision platform?

This choice plays out over the long term. Such a tool structures the territory's information system for ten years or more, so how it fits with the other software building blocks is decisive. On this point, the logic is close to that of a [guide to choosing an ERP](/en/blog/how-to-choose-erp/): compatibility with existing systems, total cost and quality of support outweigh the length of the feature list.

Three points deserve particular attention. Migrating existing data, often underestimated, is the real hidden cost of these projects. Integration with sensors and connected objects follows a logic close to the [comparison of CMMS software](/en/blog/cmms-maintenance-software-comparison/), where connecting to field equipment determines success. Finally, interoperability through open standards avoids becoming locked into a single vendor, an issue also found in the [comparison of electronic invoicing platforms](/en/blog/electronic-invoicing-platforms-comparison/).

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Choosing a closed proprietary software that makes future migration costly and complex.
2. Underestimating the budget for migrating and qualifying existing data.
3. Neglecting real business coverage and ending up with software that manages only a fraction of public policies.
