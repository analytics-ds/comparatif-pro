Centralizing employee records, payroll, leave and talent tracking in a single tool has become a priority for most French SMEs. **HR software**, also known as an HRIS (human resources information system), is gradually replacing scattered spreadsheets and email exchanges that make people management fragile once headcount passes about fifteen employees. The French market now counts over a hundred active vendors, from all-in-one suites to specialized modules.

This comparison presents the most relevant solutions by company size, details the features worth checking before committing, and offers pricing benchmarks to build a realistic budget.

## What is HR software and why adopt it in an SME

**HR software** centralizes all data and processes tied to people management: administrative records, contracts, payroll, leave and absences, annual reviews, and sometimes recruitment or training. It replaces the scattered spreadsheets and paper binders that make traceability difficult during a social audit or inspection.

Adopting such a tool brings three concrete benefits for an SME:

- **Reliable payroll and compliance**: regulatory updates (scales, contributions, mandatory filings) are handled by the vendor, reducing the risk of in-house errors.
- **Time saved on repetitive tasks**: leave approval, final settlement generation and automatic reminders free up HR staff from manual entry.
- **Visibility for management**: dashboards on payroll costs, turnover or absenteeism, useful for steering the company beyond the HR function alone.

A company still managing headcount on spreadsheets generally loses reliability once it passes twenty to thirty employees spread across several departments. This is also the stage where companies planning a broader information system benefit from comparing a dedicated HRIS with the HR modules built into an **[ERP for SMEs](/en/blog/how-to-choose-erp/)**, especially when accounting and inventory are already centralized in a single tool.

## Essential features of HR software

Scope varies significantly from one vendor to another. Before comparing pricing, it helps to distinguish core functions from optional modules.

**Core functions, present with almost every vendor:**

- Digital employee records (contracts, amendments, supporting documents).
- Leave and absence management, with an approval workflow.
- Working time tracking and scheduling for shift-based roles.
- Self-service employee portal for routine requests.

**Modules often offered as options, worth checking based on needs:**

- Payroll and social filings, sometimes handled by a dedicated module as covered in our **[best payroll software comparison](/en/blog/best-payroll-software/)**.
- Annual and professional review management.
- Recruitment (application tracking, job posting distribution).
- Talent management and training paths.

The main point of caution concerns how these modules connect: an HRIS that centralizes leave but outsources payroll to another tool forces double entry if the two systems are not linked through an API or a native connector.

## How much does HR software cost for an SME

The budget for **HR software** mainly depends on three parameters: the number of employees covered, the number of activated modules, and the level of support included in the subscription.

For basic HR functions (employee records, leave, self-service portal), the market most often ranges between 3 and 8 euros per employee per month. Adding payroll pushes the range to 5-15 euros per employee, while talent management or performance modules can double the bill for organizations that activate them.

Concretely, an SME with 30 to 50 employees that wants to cover employee records, leave and payroll should generally plan a monthly budget between 300 and 900 euros, excluding initial setup costs. Vendors often charge one-off integration fees, particularly when historical data must be migrated from a spreadsheet or a legacy system.

## How to choose HR software based on company size

The right choice mainly depends on the company's HR maturity and the tools already in place.

**1. Under 20 employees**
A spreadsheet combined with simple payroll software is often enough, provided a clear leave-approval process is formalized. A full HRIS becomes worthwhile only if the company anticipates rapid headcount growth.

**2. Between 20 and 100 employees**
This is the zone where investing in a modular HRIS (Lucca, Factorial, Eurécia) becomes most profitable: time saved on administrative tasks quickly outweighs the subscription cost. Organizations with scheduling and clocking needs tend to favor Kelio or Combo instead.

**3. Over 100 employees**
HR reporting needs, multi-site compliance, and integration with an existing **[project management tool](/en/blog/best-project-management-software/)** or ERP become decisive. Vendors able to provide open APIs and dedicated implementation support should be prioritized at this stage.

**4. Organizations with high leave seasonality**
Companies facing peaks in leave requests (back-to-school, summer period) benefit from comparing the approval workflows offered by each vendor before signing, a point detailed in our **[best leave management software comparison](/en/blog/best-leave-management-software/)**.

In every case, a free trial of two to four weeks helps validate the real usability of the self-service portal, a criterion that directly affects adoption rates among teams.

<details>
<summary>Is there free HR software for small companies?</summary>

</details>

<details>
<summary>How much does HR software cost for an SME with 30 to 50 employees?</summary>

</details>
